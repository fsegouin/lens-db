Redesign proposal thelensdb.com 2 September 2026

Every lens. Every mount. One reference.

The definitive lens database went dark in April. This is how The Lens DB takes its place, and why that is a different job from the one the site is doing today.

The opening

A reference the web relied on has disappeared

lens-db.com catalogued 8,621 lenses from manufacturer booklets and datasheets between 2012 and 2025. In April 2026 it geo-blocked Western visitors, and its owner had the Wayback Machine snapshots removed. The photographs and catalogue scans are simply gone.

In its final year it was cited on Reddit roughly weekly, and almost always for the same three jobs: telling versions apart ("Looking in Lens-DB there are two versions: L and non-L"), listing every lens for a mount, and verifying a spec. Those readers (vintage shooters, adapters, system switchers, people writing a sale listing) now have nowhere to go. That is the opening, and it will not stay open indefinitely.

The Lens DB already holds more rows than the site it replaces. What it does not yet have is the thing that made lens-db worth linking to: the feeling, on arrival, that you have reached the record of an object. The gap between "a database with a web front end" and "the reference the web cites" is what this document is about.

What we measured

Six audits, one week, no assumptions

We read the codebase, drove the live site on desktop and mobile, queried the production database read-only, mapped the competitive landscape, and audited the site for search and answer engines. Every number below is measured, not estimated.

12,076pages uncached

Every lens and camera page was served no-store and re-queried Postgres on every request, including every crawler hit.

Two causes: a geo lookup in the render path, and a missing generateStaticParams, which makes Next silently ignore the cache setting. Both fixed; see below.

0structured data

No JSON-LD, no canonical URLs, and one identical meta description across all 12,076 pages.

The site was invisible as an entity to both search engines and the AI assistants that increasingly answer "which lens" questions.

4/8searches failed

50mm 1.4, canon ef 50 1.8, sony 24-70 gm and helios 44-2 each returned nothing at all.

Punctuation was deleted rather than split, so "24-70" became "2470", and a bare "1.4" could not match "F/1.4".

89%top pages, no photo

Of the 200 most-visited lenses, 178 have no image. Overall image coverage is 16.9% of lenses and 33.5% of cameras.

Coverage is worse at the top than in the tail: the images came from recent imports, not from the vintage catalogue people actually browse.

0compatibility rows

The lens-to-camera table exists in the schema, has admin screens and an AI-chat tool, yet contains no rows. "Will this fit my camera?" is the single most common question in the evidence, and the site cannot answer it.

2human editors

37 registered users; 84% of all revisions were made by an import bot; 97% of the 254 human edits by two people. One lens has ever been submitted by the public.

There is a wiki skeleton but no community, and the edit affordances sit below six eBay listings.

One more caution that reshaped our recommendations: view counts cannot be used to prioritise. The median lens has 4 views, the maximum is 127, and the "most popular lens" on the home page is [Auto] Tamron-F 28mm F/2.8, a record whose name sorts first alphabetically. That is crawler traffic, not reader demand. Real analytics must come before any work is ranked by popularity.

Diagnosis

Five reasons the web does not link here yet

Fault 01: Citability

Nothing to cite, nothing to quote

A page becomes canonical by being quotable, stable and machine-legible. This one had no structured data, no canonical URL, no per-page description, no "last updated", no sources, and no visible identity for the object it describes.

ld+json: 0 · canonical: absent · description: 1 shared by 12,076 pages

Fault 02: The real questions

It answers the easy question, not the asked one

People ask "will it fit?", "which version is mine?", and "what is it worth?". The site answers "what are its specs", and even then only if you type the name the way the database stores it.

compatibility rows: 0 · version groups: 1 · "sony 24-70 gm" → 0 results

Fault 03: Register

It reads as a product, not a document

Near-black canvas, floating rounded cards, a marketing hero with no search field, and specifications placed below a rating strip, a price guide and six eBay cards. The page's own layout says the listings matter more than the facts.

specs begin 1,600 px down desktop · 2,133 of 3,020 px mobile

Fault 04: Substance

The records are spec sheets, not articles

No photographs on five pages in six. No prose on a third of lenses and on 2,174 of 2,187 cameras. 1,387 lenses share one of 519 identical boilerplate texts. Sources are a single scraped URL.

images 16.9% · cameras with prose 13 / 2,187

Fault 05: Renewal

No engine to keep it alive

Every comparable one-person database (lens-db, Artlimited's M42 archive, LensHero) eventually died. Without contributors and open data, this one inherits that fate, and the community has already said so in public.

37 users · 2 real editors · 1 public submission ever

Fault 06: Reach

The door is bolted to the visitors that matter

The firewall challenges everything except Googlebot and Bingbot. That blocks every AI assistant crawler, DuckDuckGo, Apple, the Internet Archive, and every link unfurler: precisely the surfaces where "which lens should I buy" is now answered.

bypassed: Googlebot, bingbot · challenged: GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Applebot, Slackbot …

Positioning

Stop competing on rows. Compete on relationships.

The open, citable registry of every lens and mount ever made, and the only place that computes what fits what.

Retailers will always win "[lens] specs". DPReview owns reviews. Wikipedia owns prose. Nobody owns the graph: mount to body, lens to adapter, version to version, price over time. lens-db had more rows and better photographs than this site, and it still died. Rows were never the moat.

Table stakes

  • 01A sourced spec sheet with a "last verified" date on every record.
  • 02Stable IDs and redirect-safe URLs; structured data on every entity.
  • 03Search that understands how photographers type.
  • 04"Lenses for [camera]" and "bodies for [lens]" as real pages.
  • 05A photograph, or a deliberate substitute, on every record.

Signature

  • 01Compatibility solver. Any lens × any body: fits, adapts (infinity? autofocus?), or impossible, derived from flange distances, with the reasoning shown.
  • 02Version and family pages. Helios 44 → 44-2 → 44M-4; Summicron v1–v5; with serial-range dating.
  • 03Open data and identifiers. A CC0 fact dump, a documented API, and a crosswalk to Wikidata, ExifTool LensID and Lensfun.
  • 04An ownership registry. "I own this": serial, price paid, condition, photograph, which together yield images, licence-clean prices and production ranges.
  • 05Announcement-day pages and a weekly digest, from the watcher that already runs.

Design direction

Field Notes

A reference work typeset like a well-made technical manual. Paper-white by default, because dense tables and long prose are what this site is for and both are worse in inverted polarity. One warm accent borrowed from lens coatings, spent only on identity and state. Names set in a grotesque, every quantity in tabular mono, so figures form a vertical rule you can scan. Nothing floats, nothing fades in.

What changes on the page

  • →Specifications move above the price guide; commerce moves into a labelled rail.
  • →A two-column article: prose left, sticky infobox and market data right; on mobile the infobox comes first.
  • →A one-sentence lead that says what the object is: the line an assistant can quote.
  • →A provenance line under every title: last edited, revisions, sale records, cite.
  • →Eleven arbitrary badge colours collapse to neutral tags plus four semantic states.

The missing photograph

Five records in six have no photograph. We prototyped a generated optical schematic to fill the gap, drawing element groups and an entrance pupil from the spec columns, and then cut it: inferred from element counts it is not the real optical formula, and a drawing that looks authoritative while being invented is exactly the trade a reference cannot make.

The absence is stated instead, carrying the facts we do hold and a prompt to contribute the picture. Filling those 7,957 gaps is a data problem, and pretending otherwise in the design would only have hidden it.

The plan

Four phases, each shippable on its own

Phase 0 Foundations

Make the pages cacheable, findable and citable. Fix search. Put the facts above the commerce. Detailed above, and complete.

Shipped 1 session
Phase 1 The article

Rebuild the entity page as a reference article, in the Field Notes direction.

  • Two-column layout with a sticky infobox; infobox first on mobile.
  • Light default, one accent, mono numerals, contrast fixes (the whole zinc-500 backlog).
  • An honest placeholder for the 83% with no photograph, and a contribution route to fix it.
  • Provenance line, sources block, "cite this page".
  • Mobile list becomes cards with a filter drawer; the 13 stacked selects go.
Next 2–3 weeks
Phase 2 The graph

Build the relationships that make it a reference rather than a table: the defensible ground.

  • Populate compatibility from mounts × bodies; add flange distances and adapter rules.
  • New page types: /cameras/[x]/lenses, /adapters/[a]-to-[b], /brands/[x], static /compare/[a]-vs-[b].
  • Version and family pages; merge the 103 duplicate groups; normalise the 17 spellings of "coverage".
  • Drop the stray /camera/ URL segment with permanent redirects.
Planned 4–6 weeks
Phase 3 The engine

Make it survive its maintainer, and make others want to link to it.

  • Open the data: CC0 fact dump, documented read API, published MCP endpoint, Wikidata / ExifTool / Lensfun crosswalk.
  • Photograph and ownership drive: "I own this" with serial, price paid and image, under a clear licence.
  • Per-field citations and a visible patrol queue; surface the 574 pending edits.
  • Announcement-day pages and a weekly digest from the existing watcher.
  • Replace the blanket challenge rule with managed bot protection so assistants and unfurlers can read the site.
Planned ongoing

Decisions we need from you

Three things we cannot decide for you

The eBay price data is the best asset and the biggest liability

Prices are the one thing no competitor has: 142,966 recorded sales behind estimates on 98% of lenses. But eBay's developer licence prohibits deriving average selling prices, retaining content after it stops being public, and, since June 2025, feeding it to generative AI, which the chat feature does. The sold-listings API is also closed to new users, and the pipeline has written no new history since 23 July.

Options: diversify to MPB/KEH plus owner-reported purchases, and treat eBay as an affiliate link rather than a data source.

The name invites a fight it does not need

"The Lens DB" is a near-clone of the brand of a site whose owner publicly resents the recreation and pulled his archive rather than share it. It also says "lenses" when a third of the ambition is cameras and mounts. Keeping it means being permanently defined as the replacement rather than the successor.

Either commit to it deliberately, or move now while the backlink profile is near zero.

Provenance of the seed corpus

98% of records cite lens-db.com as their source. Bare facts are not copyrightable, but in the EU a database can attract sui generis protection, and the descriptions are plainly someone's writing. This is also why re-sourcing each field to a manufacturer document is not merely good practice: it converts the project's biggest legal exposure into its strongest trust signal.

Re-source and rewrite as part of Phase 2, and publish an explicit sources-and-licence page.
