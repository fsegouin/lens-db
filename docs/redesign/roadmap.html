What we are building, and where it stands

The open, citable registry of every lens and mount ever made, and the only place that computes what fits what.

Not "the site with the most lens specs". Retailers win that on current gear, and lens-db.com had more rows and better photographs than this site has, and still died in April 2026 when its owner geo-blocked the West and pulled the archive. Rows were never the moat. The defensible ground is the graph between them: mount to body, lens to adapter, version to version, price over time, plus open data so the thing outlives one maintainer.

The through-line

Four phases, each one shippable on its own

Every phase makes the site more worth linking to than the last. Phase 0 made the pages legible to machines; phase 1 made them legible to people; phase 2 builds the relationships nobody else has; phase 3 makes it survivable.

Phase 0 Foundations Shipped

Make the pages cacheable, findable and citable. One session.

  • ✓Entity pages made cacheable All 12,076 were served no-store and re-queried Postgres on every hit, crawlers included. Two causes: a geo lookup in the render path and a missing generateStaticParams, which makes Next silently ignore revalidate.
  • ✓Search fixed "50mm 1.4", "canon ef 50 1.8", "sony 24-70 gm", "helios 44-2" all returned nothing. Later also "nikon z9" and "xt5", where the model name is stored with a space or hyphen.
  • ✓Structured data, canonicals, per-entity descriptions There was none: one meta description shared by 12,076 pages.
  • ✓Breadcrumbs, cross-links, honest home-page counts, a real 404

Phase 2 The graph Shipped

Build the relationships that make it a reference rather than a table. This is the positioning.

  • ✓Camera URLs cleaned 2,181 pages lived at /cameras/camera/<name>. Done early because the cost grows with every link earned.
  • ✓"Lenses for this camera" pages, about 1,700 of them The most frequent question in the research, which the site could not answer.
  • ✓Brand pages, 266 of them, plus the brand column normalised
  • ✓318 duplicate lenses merged, and 1,662 double-counted sales removed The twins had recorded the same eBay sales twice, so prices were computed from inflated evidence.
  • ✓Mount facts: flange distance per mount, 76 of 132 The figures were already in the corpus, embedded in a spec string on 6,708 lenses. Two passes read them out, including the mounts the source spells under another name, and three were filled by hand. T-mount and Tamron Adaptall stay blank on purpose: they ship with a swappable rear adapter, so no single register describes them.
  • ✓The adapter solver A matrix of 34 lens mounts against the 8 shortest-register bodies, and 289 pair pages that state the verdict and show the register arithmetic so the answer can be checked. Verified against known pairings: Canon EF to Nikon F needs corrective glass, Sony E to Fujifilm X is too tight at 0.3 mm.
  • ✓Static comparison pages, 3,023 of them A pair earns a page when the two are genuinely cross-shopped: same mount, same focal length, within a stop, and each traded at least 20 times. Recorded sales are the one real-world signal this database owns, since only 54 comparisons have ever been recorded and view counts top out at 127.
  • ✓Price estimates re-derived for the 353 merged survivors Their evidence changed underneath them when the duplicate sales went. 165 landed on different figures. A $5 floor now keeps parts listings from setting a headline price: a Mamiya-Sekor C 127mm read "$2" against a $190 median.
  • ✓Controlled vocabularies settled coverage went from 17 spellings to 5, era from 12 to 2, production status from 13 to 4. These columns are filtered with exact equality, so every extra spelling was a filter returning nothing: the Medium Format option matched no lens at all, because every medium format lens was written with a capital F. A shutter mechanism recorded in 476 cameras' body_type moved to a column of its own, so those pages stop asserting a body style the record never held.
  • ○The eBay classifier attributes cheap listings to expensive variants Surfaced by the merge: a Minolta MD Shift 35mm F/2.8 carries thirteen sub-$150 sales that are plain non-shift 35mm listings, dragging its median from $761 to $120. The merge and the estimate are both right; the classification underneath is not.

Waiting on you

Four things I cannot decide or fix alone

The price pipeline is dead, and prices are the best asset

No new sale has been recorded since 23 July, six weeks ago, though the estimates were re-stamped on 31 August. It is blocked on capturing an eBay sign-in. Every day this runs, the one thing no competitor has gets staler.

The firewall shuts out the audience the goal depends on

Everything except Googlebot and Bingbot is challenged: every AI assistant crawler, DuckDuckGo, Apple, the Internet Archive, and every link unfurler. Those are exactly the surfaces where "which lens should I buy" now gets answered. A dashboard change, and the cheapest high-leverage move available.

eBay's licence and the price data

The developer licence forbids deriving average selling prices, retaining content once it stops being public, and, since June 2025, feeding it to generative AI, which the chat feature does. Phase 2 puts more weight on this data, so the question stops being deferrable.

The name

"The Lens DB" is a near-clone of a brand whose owner resents the recreation. The backlink profile is near zero today, which is the cheapest moment to change it, and the most expensive moment to keep it is after phase 3 succeeds.
